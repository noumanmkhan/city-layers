Vote By Mail and Online Specimen Ballots Now Available for November 3 General Election

Fri, 10/02/2026
Press Release
Vote By Mail

Sample Ballots Now Online for Viewing

Chicago voters can now view their precinct-specific sample ballot for the 2026 General Election through the voter lookup tool on chicagoelections.gov. 

Reviewing ballots before Election Day helps voters become familiar with the offices candidates and public policy questions that they will decide. The 2026 General Election includes the offices of Governor and Lieutenant Governor of Illinois, members of the U.S. Congress, Cook County and judicial offices, President and Member of the Chicago Board of Education, and others. Voters will also consider several referendum questions concerning public policy, some specific to their Wards or precincts. 

Sample ballots for the General Election also are available in English, Spanish, Chinese, Hindi, Polish, Korean and Tagalog. 

Vote By Mail Ballots Shipped Via U.S. Postal Service 

As of October 1, 2026, the Chicago Board of Elections has mailed approximately 210,500 Vote By Mail ballots for the 2026 General Election. 

Voters who receive a Vote By Mail ballot should carefully follow the instructions included with the ballot and return their completed ballot in the sealed and certified ballot-return envelope as soon as possible. Vote By Mail ballots must be returned to the Chicago Board of Elections by the close of polls on Election Day, November 3, unless returned by mail. Ballots postmarked on or before Election Day may be counted if they are received by the Board within 14 days after Election Day. Ballots postmarked after Election Day cannot be counted under Illinois law. 

Voters who choose not to return their ballot through the U.S. Postal Service may instead return their sealed and certified ballot-return envelope through a Chicago Board of Elections secured drop box. Secured drop boxes are available to Chicago voters at the Chicago Board of Elections Voting Supersite at 137 S. State Street and in the lobby of the Board's office at 69 W. Washington Street. The drop boxes have unique locks and tamper-evident seals and are monitored by full-time security cameras and election officials during Early Voting and Election Day hours.
